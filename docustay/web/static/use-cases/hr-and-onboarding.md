## Three steps for a new person.

**Offer letter.** Role, pay and start date, for the candidate to accept.

**New hire paperwork.** The forms to fill on day one, in one send.

**To a whole group.** Send to a list from a spreadsheet, on every plan.

## How it goes.

For example, a shop sends an offer letter and the first-day paperwork together, and the new hire signs both from a phone the night before they start.

1. **Offer**: Offer letter
2. **Accept**: Candidate signs
3. **Paperwork**: First-day forms

## An offer letter and a first-day pack.

One goes to a candidate to accept, the other to a new hire to fill in before day one.

**Offer letter template.** Role, pay and start date, for a candidate to accept. Employer and Candidate each sign their own boxes, over 3 pages and 26 fields, about 10 minutes in all.

**New hire paperwork.** The forms to fill on day one, in one send. Company and New starter each sign their own boxes, over 6 pages and 50 fields, about 16 minutes in all.

## What the document asks for.

Read from the template itself, so this list is what the signer will see.

**What the offer letter asks for.** Company name, Date of this letter, Candidate’s full name, Company’s legal name, Job title, Manager’s name, Start date, Place of work, or remote, Pay, with how often, How often you are paid, Classification, Paid time off, Hours or schedule, Background check, and 12 more. Each box sits where it belongs on the page and belongs to one person, so nobody fills in a part that is not theirs.

**What the new hire paperwork asks for.** Company name, Your full name, Start date, Role, Manager’s name, the people team contact, the IT contact, I have received and read the staff handbook, Handbook initials, I understand that I must treat colleagues, customers and suppliers with respect, and that harassment and discrimination , the person for raising concerns, I will keep the company’s and its customers’ private information private, during and after my employment, Privacy initials, I agree, and 36 more. Each box sits where it belongs on the page and belongs to one person, so nobody fills in a part that is not theirs.

## Before you send the paperwork.

Paperwork should not be the first thing a new person does on their first morning.

**Send early, send once.** The new hire pack gathers the usual paperwork into one send, so the person gets one email and fills everything in from a phone before they start.

**Keep the offer simple.** An offer letter states the role, the pay and the start date and asks for one signature. Anything long belongs in the agreement that follows, not in the offer.

## While the person decides.

A candidate with an offer is waiting on you as much as you are waiting on them.

**Nothing to install.** The candidate opens the link, reads the offer and signs, on a phone or a computer, without making an account.

**Reminders that are not awkward.** Reminders go out automatically until the person has signed, so you are not the one who has to ask twice.

**Send a batch.** When several people start together, send one template to a list from a CSV and each person gets their own copy to sign.

## After the person signs.

Hand off the signed copy and move on to the first day.

**A sealed copy for the file.** The signed PDF is sealed and carries a certificate of completion, ready to go into the person's file.

**Hear about it in your own tools.** A signed document can raise a signed webhook event, so your own systems know to start the next step.

**Team roles.** Give the people who send offers a role, and keep who can see what under control. Pro is one flat price for the whole workspace, and Pay as you go is $5 per team member a month.

## Questions

**Can the candidate sign on a phone?**

Yes. No app and no account.

**Can a manager approve first?**

Yes. Add an approver before the candidate.

**Is anything sent for me?**

No. Nothing goes out until you press send.
