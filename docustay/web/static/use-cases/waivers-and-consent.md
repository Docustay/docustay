## The documents people collect most.

**Liability waiver.** Risk and responsibility, signed before an activity.

**Photo and media release.** Permission to use pictures, recordings and names.

**Permission slip.** A parent or guardian agrees to an activity.

## How it goes.

For example, a climbing gym emails its waiver to each guest before their visit, each guest signs on their own phone, and the gym can find any signed waiver by name.

1. **Send**: To each guest
2. **Sign**: On their phone
3. **Saved**: A record each
4. **Find**: By name

## Four documents for activities, pictures and children.

Each can be sent to a whole list, so every person gets their own copy to sign.

**Photo release form.** Permission to use someone's picture. Subject signs, over 2 pages and 16 fields, about 7 minutes in all.

**Liability waiver template.** Risk and responsibility, signed before an activity. Participant signs, over 2 pages and 9 fields, about 4 minutes in all.

**Media release form.** A guardian lets you use a child's image and name. Parent or guardian signs, over 3 pages and 19 fields, about 8 minutes in all.

## Before you send it.

A waiver is only as useful as the record behind it, so decide what you will keep.

**A list, not one at a time.** Upload the guest list and send the waiver to everyone at once. Each person gets their own copy and signs it, and each signature becomes its own signed record.

**Minors need a guardian.** For anyone under 18, use the media release or permission slip, where a parent or guardian signs on the child's behalf. Say clearly in the document whose permission is being given.

The point of a waiver is being able to show it later.

**One sealed record per person.** Every signature is sealed on its own PDF with a certificate, so you can find one person's waiver without sorting through a pile.

**Export what happened.** The audit log records each step and exports as a CSV, which is handy for an insurer or a venue that asks for proof.
