## A scope for the job and a record for each change.

Two documents agencies reach for again and again, one for the work and one for when the work moves.

**Statement of work template.** What will be done, by when, for how much. Agency and Client each sign their own boxes, over 6 pages and 77 fields, about 26 minutes in all.

**Change order template.** A signed change to the scope or the price. Provider and Client each sign their own boxes, over 3 pages and 24 fields, about 9 minutes in all.

**Payment plan agreement template.** Payments by date, written into the agreement. Business and Customer each sign their own boxes, over 3 pages and 29 fields, about 11 minutes in all.

**Invoice approval form.** A signed yes before an invoice is paid. Sender and Approver each sign their own boxes, over 2 pages and 23 fields, about 8 minutes in all.

Read from the template itself, so this list is what the signer will see.

**What the statement of work asks for.** Project name, Date of this SOW, Agency’s legal name, Client’s legal name, Date of the master agreement, if any, State the objective in a few sentences, Say how the Client will know it worked, Start date, End date, List what the Agency is assuming, the Agency’s project lead, How often the sides check in, the Client’s project lead, Review period, and 63 more. ## Before you send the scope.

Disputes about extra work almost always start with a scope that was never written down.

**One scope per job.** Use the statement of work for each job under a larger agreement, so every job has its own deliverables, dates and price, signed by the client.

**Describe it instead of filling it in.** If a job does not fit a template, describe it in plain words and Scribe writes the document and places the fields; you review it before it is sent. Scribe never sends on its own.

## While the client reviews.

The client is often busy, and the quicker the scope is signed the sooner the work can start.

**The client signs anywhere.** A link on a phone or a computer, no account, and a signature drawn or typed.

**Reminders on autopilot.** Reminders keep going until the client has signed, without you writing the third nudge.

**Send from your own system.** If your project tool already knows the client and the job, send the template from code and hear about the result through a signed webhook.

## When the scope changes.

The change order is the document that keeps the first agreement honest.

**Name the agreement it changes.** A change order records what changes, what it costs and when, and refers back to the agreement it changes. Send it before the extra work begins, not after.

**Get paid inside the document.** A payment field can take a deposit as the last step of signing, through your own Stripe account. The fee is 2% on Free and Pay as you go and 0% on Pro, on top of Stripe's.

**One price for the studio.** Pro is $35 a month flat for the whole workspace, so adding another account manager never changes the bill.
