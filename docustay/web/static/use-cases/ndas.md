## Three things for the start of a deal.

**NDA.** Both parties promise to keep what they share private.

**Non-solicitation agreement.** A promise not to hire away each other's people.

**In your own name.** Email carries your logo and your name.

They open the link, read it, tick that they agree to sign electronically and finish.

## An NDA and a promise not to recruit.

The two documents most deals start with, both already set up for two signers.

**NDA template.** Both sides keep what they share private. Party A and Party B each sign their own boxes, over 3 pages and 25 fields, about 8 minutes in all.

**Non-solicitation agreement.** A promise not to hire away each other's people. Company and Employee each sign their own boxes, over 3 pages and 14 fields, about 6 minutes in all.

## What the document asks for.

Read from the template itself, so this list is what the signer will see.

**What the nda asks for.** First party’s name, Second party’s name, Effective date, Party A’s legal name, Party A’s address, Party B’s legal name, Party B’s address, the purpose, in a sentence or two, Sharing period, Protection period, Governing law (state), Printed name, Title or capacity, Party A signature, and 11 more. Each box sits where it belongs on the page and belongs to one person, so nobody fills in a part that is not theirs.

**What the non-solicitation agreement asks for.** Company name, Employee’s full name, Company’s legal name, Date, Employee’s role, Period after leaving, Governing law (state), Printed name, Employee signature, Employee date signed, Title or capacity, Company signature, Company date signed, and 1 more. Each box sits where it belongs on the page and belongs to one person, so nobody fills in a part that is not theirs.

## The three decisions an NDA turns on.

An NDA is short, so the details that matter are few. Settle them before you send.

**One-way or mutual.** A mutual NDA binds both sides to the same promise, which is easier to ask for and quicker to agree. If only one side will share secrets, change the wording so it says so before you send it.

**Where the other side signs.** Many NDAs are signed by someone who is travelling or between meetings. The signing page works on a phone with no account, so you are not waiting for a laptop.

## While you wait for the other side.

An NDA is usually urgent: the conversation cannot start until it is signed.

**Send in your own name.** The email carries your logo and goes out under your name, so the other side sees a message from you and not from a stranger.

**Choose the order.** You can have the other side sign first, or both sign at once. Everyone at once is the fastest way to get the document back.

**Know when it is opened.** The document shows when it was opened and by whom, so a quiet signer is a reminder you can send, not a mystery.

## What you keep after both sides sign.

If a dispute ever comes up, the record is what you will want.

**A sealed copy with a certificate.** The signed PDF is sealed so a later change shows, and its last page records who signed and when, with a hash of the file.

**Proof anyone can check.** The verify page checks a signed PDF in the browser, with no account. You can give it to the other side or an adviser.

**A note on what it is.** A Docustay signature is a simple electronic signature with a strong record and a digital seal. The template is a starting point in plain words and is not legal advice.

## Questions

**Who signs first?**

You choose. One at a time, or both together.

**Can the other side sign on a phone?**

Yes. They open the link and sign, with no account.

**Is the NDA legal advice?**

No. It is a starting point in plain words. Change what does not fit before you send it.
