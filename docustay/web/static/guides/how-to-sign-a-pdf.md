# How to sign a PDF

Three ways, from the quickest to the one that keeps proof.

## Pick the one that fits.

**Someone sent you a link.** Open it, fill in your fields, type or draw your signature, tick that you agree to sign electronically and finish. You do not need an account.

**You need to sign your own file.** Upload the PDF, place your signature field and sign it yourself, then send it on.

**You need others to sign.** Upload the PDF, name who signs, and send it. Everyone gets the sealed copy with its certificate.

## What a good signed PDF has.

**Every signature.** Each person's name and the time they signed, on the page.

**A seal.** A digital signature over the whole file, so a later change shows.

**A way to check.** Anyone can drop the file on the verify page with no account.

## Which way to sign a PDF fits you.

The right one depends on who else has to sign.

**Only you.** Put a picture of your signature on a copy of the file with the free sign a PDF tool. It runs in your browser and the file is never uploaded, so it suits a form that only needs your name.

**You and others.** Upload the PDF, say who signs and in what order, and send it. Each person signs on their own device with no account, and everyone gets the sealed copy.

**From your own software.** If your system already holds the document, send it from code with a template and a list of signers, and hear about the result through a signed webhook.

## The record matters as much as the picture.

A signature image proves very little on its own.

**A seal over the whole file.** The finished PDF is sealed with a digital signature over the whole file, so any later change shows.

**A certificate of completion.** The last page lists who signed and when, the address each act came from, and a hash of the file.

**A check anyone can run.** The verify page checks a signed PDF in the browser with no account, so you can hand it to the other side or an adviser.

## Eight things people ask when a PDF will not cooperate.

Each answer is about what the free tool and the app actually do.

**It is a scan.** A scan is a picture of a page, so you place your signature on top of it like any other page. It works in the free tool and when sending.

**I am on a phone.** Choose the file, draw your signature with a finger and tap the page where it goes. Signers who receive a document sign the same way, with nothing to install.

**The file is large.** The free tool opens files up to 150 MB, because it never uploads them. When sending, uploads are limited to 25 MB on Free and 100 MB on Pro.

**It is not a PDF.** Docustay only accepts PDF files, so save or export a Word or image file as a PDF first. You can also write a document as HTML and make a template from it.

**Several people must sign.** Name each person and choose whether they sign one at a time or all at once. Each box on the page belongs to one person, so nobody fills in someone else's part.

**I need proof later.** Keep the sealed copy with its certificate. The audit log records every step and exports as a CSV if you are ever asked for the history.

**The form needs filling first.** Use the free fill a PDF tool to type into the form's own fields, then add your signature with the sign a PDF tool. Both run in your browser.

**I made a mistake.** Both tools write a new copy and never change the file you chose, so you can go back to the original and start again whenever you like.

## Questions

**Is a typed signature valid?**

It is a simple electronic signature with a record of intent. Whether it is enough for a given document depends on the document and the place. See legal validity.

**Can I sign on my phone?**

Yes. The signing page works on any phone or computer.

**Can I sign a Word file?**

Save it as a PDF first. Docustay only accepts PDF files.
